Expense Desk · Level 2 example solution · architecture

An AI that decides most claims and calls a person only at defined gates

A fixed workflow does the cheap, predictable work. An agent loop takes the claims that need investigation. Code, not the model, does the arithmetic and enforces the safety rules. A gate table you can read in one file decides when a manager or Finance has to look. The AI never sends money: approved claims are handed to Finance's system.

The pipeline, nine stages

The employee submits a small form (amount, date, category, note) and a photo of the receipt. Everything below runs when "Process with AI" is pressed.

What each stage does

1 · Intake and hard checks

The employee's form is already JSON: amount, expense date, category, note. Code rejects immediately if the claim is past 60 days (R5), has no receipt image over $25 (R1), or repeats an earlier submission. No model call, so these cost nothing.

2 · Read the receipt

A vision call reads the photo and fills a fixed shape: merchant, date, each line item tagged (alcohol, meal, hotel night, gift), total, receipt number, guest count. It must be structured because code does the math next. The same call reads the note for purpose: travel, hosting a client, or none.

3 · Cross-check

Code compares what the employee typed with what the receipt shows: amount, date, category. It also checks the line items add up to the total and that the receipt number was never paid. The employee's trips, past claims and manager are fetched here, in parallel. These are plain lookups by id, not search.

4 · Flag and route

A list of small if-checks, each returning a flag with a severity: note says "part 3", no trip covers a hotel night, "gift card" on the receipt, form and receipt disagree. No flags: policy engine. Any real flag: the agent, which is told why it was called.

5a · Policy engine (code)

Applies the $75 meal cap, removes alcohol, takes the lower of claimed and receipt total, prices hotel nights against $250. The routine claim's decision comes from arithmetic, not from a model's guess.

5b · Investigator agent

For claims whose steps can't be scripted. It starts with everything already gathered plus the flags, uses its tools until it can decide, then submits. It can read and calculate. It cannot pay.

6 · Check before acting

Three checks. Guardrails: sanity rules (pay no more than the claim or the receipt, $0 on a reject, real rule ids). Gates: the table below decides who must sign. Second opinion: a separate model call, no tools, asks "would you pay this?" for agent-path claims and payouts over $200.

7 · Write-up

One model call turns the decision into a short summary for the reviewer and an email to the employee. When a person will decide, it drafts the email for both outcomes so the final message is ready after the click.

Inside the agent loop

When a human is required

These business scenarios live in one file, server/src/ai/gates.ts, as a list with an id, a plain-English description, a reviewer and a test. Thresholds sit in one config object. Changing company policy means editing this table, not the prompts.

GateTriggers whenReviewer
AMOUNT_OVER_LIMITAmount to pay is over $500 (R6)Manager
POLICY_ESCALATIONA rule says "send to Finance": gift or cash equivalent (R7), possible splitting (R9), hotel with no matching trip (R4)Finance
LOW_CONFIDENCEThe decision to pay has model confidence under 0.75Finance
VERIFIER_DISAGREESThe independent second opinion would not pay as proposedFinance
AGENT_INCONCLUSIVEThe agent hit its turn or token cap, or returned an invalid decisionFinance
HIGH_RISK_SIGNALA high-severity flag survives: receipt contradicts the form (amount, date or category), line items don't add up, near-duplicate of a paid claimFinance
JUST_UNDER_LIMITPayout is $475–$500, inside 5% below the manager limit (possible threshold dodging)Manager

If a claim hits both kinds of gate, Finance reviews it. Every triggered gate and its reason is stored with the claim and shown on its review card. "Notify" means the queue count updates and a message to the reviewer is written to the server log, since the lab has no email service.

Design decisions to know about

  • Finance outranks the manager. A claim over $500 that is also flagged for doubt goes to Finance alone, not through a two-step chain, because the app has no reviewer identity.
  • Escalations enter the same queue. Finance cases appear as cards too, with the amount they would approve (or none).
  • The second opinion is selective. Agent-path claims and payouts over $200 only, so boring claims stay at a few cents.
  • Failures retry three times, then wait in the failed payouts queue for a person to press Retry. No background scheduler.
